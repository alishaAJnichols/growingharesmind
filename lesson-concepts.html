European hare

Bundle the clover

Counting & place value

Loose clover leaves scatter across the field. Every time ten collect in one place, they cinch together into a single bundle — ones quietly becoming a ten right in front of the child, with no number or word required to show what just happened.

White-tailed jackrabbit

Join the trails

Addition & subtraction

Two separate hop-trails across the plain glow and fuse into one combined path — that's addition. Drag along a trail and watch a section lift away and vanish — that's subtraction. Same open landscape, two directions of the same motion.

Snowshoe hare

Print the snow

Multiplication

A single row of paw prints presses into fresh snow. Drag it downward and it repeats itself, row after identical row, filling the field. The array of prints IS the multiplication — count the rows, count a row, see how the whole field fills.

Arctic hare

Share the den

Fractions

The arctic hare's snow den splits into equal wedge-shaped rooms. One room glows while the rest stay plain — that one lit room, out of all the equal rooms, is the fraction. No numerator, no denominator symbol needed to see "this much out of this many."

Woolly hare

Trace the ridge

Geometry

The woolly hare climbs a jagged mountain skyline. Trace between three peaks and a triangle lights up against the ridge behind it — angles, sides, and shapes discovered by literally walking the terrain, not by naming them.
